'Tis the time of the year for reflecting and posting about it on social media! ✨

One of the things I've been thinking about doing for a little while is a run down of the "things I give money to because I think they're good".

Goal: I think we need to get out of this capitalistic mindset of only paying for things when we're forced to, and switch to supporting the things we want to see continue and flourish. To that end, I want to demonstrate that it is possible to do this now, encourage others to do this where they can, and shout about some specific projects that _I_ think we should be supporting.

Privilege note: we live in a society which squeezes many people to the extent that they don't have much choice about where they put their money - they have to spend their money in a specific set of ways _in order to survive_. So, I'm in no way saying "you should be doing this and if you don't/can't then you're a bad person". I'd love to see a future where people _do_ have choice/power/agency over what projects they financially support, and where that collective power is what shapes the progression of our collective society. And I'm a big proponent of "living our eutopia" (working out what our personal eutopia looks like, and seeing how close we can get to that right now if we reach) so I'm gonna try it - I'm gonna use the (limited but non-zero) power I have in my pocket to support the projects that I want to see sustain and grow.

The straw that broke the camel's back this time was this post: https://ravenation.club/@etherdiver/110832554098802166
I've been seeing posts for a while talking about how Spotify is shit and how if we want people to be making music we need to be getting money to them some other way. So I bought some music for the first time in years, and while listening to it on my phone (via VLC) was objectively indistinguishable I'm sure - it felt delightfully sweet somehow.

I'm also a bit of a contrarian sometimes, and so the simple fact that it's unusual for people to talk about their money (You should talk to your colleagues about how much you and they earn - it's only in the bosses interests for money to be a private topic) makes me want to do it.
